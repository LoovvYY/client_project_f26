Starting Your Experience

Turn your project ideas into shared commitments. This stage focuses on professional communication, productive first meetings, and agreements that help your team and partner work together.

Still exploring your goals or the community context? Review Preparing for Your Experience before contacting your partner.

Build trust from the start

First impressions help shape a partnership. Clear team commitments, a shared meeting agenda, and an agreed project scope can build trust with your partner and help teammates feel comfortable asking questions. You do not need every answer at the beginning; use early conversations to clarify uncertainty together.

Focus on three starting goals

  • Partner communication: Write clear, professional outreach to begin your working relationship.
  • First meetings: Use a structured kickoff meeting to discuss roles, expectations, and project boundaries.
  • Shared agreements: Document team commitments and the scope agreed upon with your partner.

Contact your partner and prepare for the first meeting

  1. Prepare your introduction. Use the Initial Client Email Examples to draft your first message. Adapt the message to your team, course, and partner.
  2. Share an agenda. Use the Initial Client Meeting Sample Agenda to organize the discussion and invite the partner to add priorities.
  3. Discuss the work together. Clarify the partner's needs, team roles, expected outcomes, and project boundaries.
  4. Confirm what you agreed. Record decisions, open questions, and next actions so everyone has the same understanding.

Document the partnership

Use partnership resources to discuss expectations and responsibilities before work moves forward. Review the agreement with the people involved, and identify any questions that need clarification.

Student organizations working with ELO can use the Student Org-ELO Partnership Agreement Template and review the SO-ELL Schedule for relevant milestones. Confirm which materials apply to your course or program.

Before moving into active project work

Use this brief check to identify anything your team still needs to discuss.

  • We have introduced ourselves and agreed on how to communicate.
  • We have discussed the project scope, roles, and expected deliverables.
  • We have recorded our commitments and open questions.
  • We know our next actions and the relevant milestones.
